Worried about someone? Tell us

For any worry about the safety or wellbeing of a child or adult at Kingswood United, tell the welfare team. No concern is too small, and you don't need proof.

What happens after you raise a concern

  1. Your concern reaches the welfare team through the report form. If you called, texted or emailed, the person you contacted completes the form for you.
  2. It is checked straight away for immediate danger. If anyone is at immediate risk, the emergency services are called first.
  3. It goes to the welfare contact for the age group involved, who records it and owns it from then on.
  4. You get an acknowledgement within 5 days.
  5. The welfare contact decides how it needs to be handled:
    • Club level. Poor practice or a lower-level concern, dealt with inside the club.
    • County FA advice. A possible risk of harm, or poor practice that keeps happening. The club consults the East Riding County FA Designated Safeguarding Officer.
    • Referred on. A serious safeguarding concern or a disclosure of abuse, referred immediately to the County FA, children's social care or the police.
  6. The people who need to know are told. That can include parents or carers, the County FA, and social workers.
  7. A concern is only closed once Alex, as Lead Welfare Officer, has checked that the record is complete, the agreed actions have happened and the people involved have been updated.

What you share is handled sensitively and passed only to people who need it to keep someone safe. Nobody at the club can promise to keep a safeguarding concern secret, because it may need to be shared with the County FA, social care or the police to protect someone. Records are kept securely, with access limited to the welfare team and a small number of committee members.

The full process is in the Escalations Policy and the Escalations Pathway.

If you're a player

If anything at football makes you feel worried, upset, scared or uncomfortable, tell a grown-up you trust. It is never your fault and you won't be in trouble for speaking up.

  • Tell your mum, dad or carer, or tell your coach.
  • The welfare team's job is to help keep everyone at the club safe and happy. Your coach or parent can help you talk to them.
  • You can tell them about something happening to you or to a friend.
  • If you're 16 or over, or play in one of our adult teams, you can contact Charlotte or any of the welfare team yourself.
  • If you'd rather talk to someone outside the club, call Childline free on 0800 1111 at any time. Adults can call Samaritans free on 116 123.
  • If someone online makes you uncomfortable, you can report it to CEOP.

If a child tells you something

Stay calm and listen. Your job is to pass it on, not to work out whether it's true.

  • Reassure them they have done the right thing by telling you.
  • Explain that you need to tell someone who can help. Don't promise to keep it secret.
  • Don't ask leading questions, investigate, or confront anyone.
  • Write down what was said as soon as you can, using the child's own words.
  • Report it the same day, using the report form or by contacting the welfare team.

For parents and carers

We encourage every parent and carer to take England Football's free online course, Safeguarding Awareness for Parents & Carers. It takes about an hour and covers what safeguarding means in football, the signs of abuse and grooming, how to check a club's safeguarding policies, and how to report a concern.

Concerns about an adult

Kingswood United has adult teams and disabled players. Safeguarding covers adults too, including any adult who may be less able to protect themselves from harm.

  • Report a concern about an adult in the same way: use the report form, or contact Charlotte or any of the welfare team.
  • If an adult is in immediate danger, call 999.
  • Hull Multi Agency Safeguarding Hub (adults) 01482 616 092 adultsafeguarding@hullcc.gov.uk Hull City Council's team for concerns about an adult at risk of abuse or neglect.

Call 999 if in any doubt.

Kinloss Garth

Address to give
Kinloss Garth Sports Field, Kinloss Garth, Hull HU7 4LY
Defibrillator
On the wall next to the Tea Bar. Access code C159X.
Ambulance access
The main gate at the bottom of Kinloss Garth. Send an adult to the gate to meet the crew, and keep the yellow hatched area clear.
First aid kit
Every coach or manager carries one to every session and fixture.
Head, neck or back injury
Don't move the casualty. Wait for the ambulance.
Children
Never leave a child alone. If a child goes to hospital before a parent arrives, a DBS-checked coach or team official goes with them.
Missing child
Search the site and contact the parent or carer. If the child isn't found within 15 minutes, or you have any concern for their safety, call 999 and tell the welfare team.
Thunder or lightning
Stop play and take shelter. Don't restart until 30 minutes after the last thunder or lightning.
Fire or evacuation
Assembly point: the garages on Kinloss Garth, on the left outside the main gates.
